# Anatomical and Functional ROIs

## Defining spatial boundaries

Region of interest analysis allows you to test specific neuroanatomical hypotheses with higher statistical power. Regions can be defined anatomically using standardized probabilistic atlases, or functionally using an independent localizer scan. The choice depends on whether your hypothesis targets an anatomical structure or a functionally selective area.

## Anatomical precision vs functional heterogeneity

Anatomical masks from the Harvard-Oxford atlas are objective, reproducible, and completely independent of experimental task activation. However, anatomical boundaries do not always align with functional boundaries. Functional localizers capture individual functional topography, but require dedicated scanning time and separate localizer tasks.

## ROI selection checklist

Decide on your region of interest definition before inspecting the statistical maps of your experiment. Use standardized atlases for structural targets, and independent localizers when investigating specialized functional areas.
